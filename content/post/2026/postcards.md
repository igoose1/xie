I learned about [snail mail][define] in 2013 when I was in a scout camp at Lake Baikal. I'm a child of this century, I didn't know how to communicate without the Internet. So when a girl suggested to exchange addresses of our houses because her village didn't have a network connection, I agreed but didn't believe it would work. But it did! We were sending sheets of papers in envelopes, putting stamps, waiting months and receiving a reply. I learned you can decorate these sheets with markers and stickers instead of writing it with a graphite pencil. I also learned that the girl had to walk to the post office to check her mails and distance could be a challenge in the winter. That was beautiful while it lasted.

[define]: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/snail-mail

It broke one day when I simply didn't write an answer. That was bad, I agree, okay?? Only many years later I found old envelopes with her address and send a new letter with excuses and ways to contact me online. Even though she moved out, she received the letter the same season and contacted me in social media.

In 2022 I started to travel in Southeast Asia. I visited big cities and less popular towns, and I started to send postcards to friends and family in other countries. 

A postcard is a little piece of thick paper. One side is usually illustrated and another side is divided into two parts: a message and an addressee's details. In many countries I visited, sending a postcard costs less than a glass of tap water in a cafe. Limited space also encourages you to write short and simple.

Receiving a postcard is so fun! This asymmetric exchange (easy to send, joyful to receive) motivates me to hunt for new illustrations and send more postcards. Every time I have guests coming, I encourage them to send a postcard back too.

If you got interested, that's a simple FAQ to show how simple this is.

## FAQ

### Where to buy a postcard?

Find them in book stores or gift shops. If your town is a bit touristic, go to the street every tourist visits. You will find plenty of postcards there.

### How to send?

For the first sending, go to your local post office. These are still brick-and-mortar. Explain to the worker that you want to send a postcard and ask about a fee to its destination. They will help you to purchase necessary stamps. I'll spare the details how to stick the stamp, that's another symbolic action you should never skip.

### How to spell an address?

I usually write an address in its local language and then write a country with highlighted English letters. So if I had a friend working at the restaurant in Athens, I'd write:

> Μαύρο Άσπρο άλογο, Απόλλωνος, Αθήνα, 10557
> **GREECE**

### What to write?

Not helping here! As a hint, you can write where you are now and how you got there. It doesn't even matter. Once I sent a postcard with one Russian word on it and it's still memorable.
